# Interface conventions

OpenOrc uses one tile ground for the sidebar and conversation, white planes for the work that needs its own room, and fill steps instead of borders and shadows. Shared components and semantic tokens keep conversations, task documents, and review panels consistent. The app-scoped [design record](../apps/desktop/DESIGN.md) lists the tokens and component recipes.

## Tokens and appearance

Color palettes are defined in `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/lib/theme-palettes.ts` and applied by `lib/theme.ts`. Components read semantic roles from `app.css`, never palette literals: `--bg` is the tile under the sidebar, the conversation, and page toolbars; `--surface` is the plane under the work panel, sheets, transcript cards, and inputs (darker than the tile in dark mode); `--surface-2` is the fill (hover, chips, tracks, fields); `--surface-3` is the strong fill (press, hover on a filled control, off tracks, meters); `--line` divides rows at `--hairline` width. `--ink` through `--ink-4` carry hierarchy; every role holds 4.5:1 on tile, plane, and fill, and faint text never sits on the strong fill. `--accent` and `--accent-fg` make the one ink pill per screen. `--ok`/`--bad` mark diffs and results, `--attention` colors dots, `--warn` is warning text.

Two contextual roles follow the ground they sit on. `--current-row-fill` (selected rows) and `--control-fill` (secondary pills and chips) are `--navigation-soft` and `--surface` on the tile; every plane restates them as `--surface-2`, and a tile card inside a plane restates them back to `--surface`. Mark a new plane with `data-plane` rather than adding it to the selector lists. Floating layers paint `--float-fill`: the plane over the tile and the tile over a plane, read under the placed layer by `lib/use-anchor-ground.ts`.

OpenOrc has its own tile, plane, and translucent ink fills; Cursor keeps its own grays; flat light palettes have a hand-tuned ground step so planes separate. Fill and line roles accept `#rrggbbaa`. Product presets keep their source colors. No palette paints a gradient: the sidebar, the work column and the panel share one ground color. [Theme references](theme-references.md) record the evidence and adaptations. Light, dark, and system appearance are independent of palette selection. Verify both modes, a second palette, and saved custom colors when changing a component.

Appearance separates action, navigation, and content accents. Use `--accent` and its companions for the primary action, `--navigation-ink`/`--navigation-soft` for selected destinations and rows, and `--content-ink`/`--content-soft` for links and inline code. Each palette defines its own defaults: Claude uses clay actions and blue content accents; GitHub uses green primary actions and blue links, following [Primer's color roles](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/); Eliron keeps graphite controls, pink navigation, and blue links. Accent combinations and custom role colors are stored per palette and mode. Editing or resetting Actions leaves Content intact; resetting all accents preserves unrelated overrides.

Typography, radii, control heights, and motion are `@theme static` tokens in `app.css`. The interface uses one regular weight; bold appears only for author emphasis inside replies, documents, and comments. UI text is 12, 13, or 14px, with 16, 22, and 28px headings and tabular 18/22 and 16/20 numerals. The interface font is an Appearance setting (Immaculate Gothic by default, Inter, Geist, or the system font), mapped in `fonts.css`. Code has its own setting (JetBrains Mono, Geist Mono, or System monospace) applied through `--font-mono` to code blocks, editors, diffs, and open terminals; terminals refit after the font loads without restarting their shell.

Corner radii are chosen per component: 10 for rows and menu items, 16 for drop menus and code blocks, 18 for popovers and prompt bubbles, 20 for other sheets, dialogs, and the composer, 24 for the New thread composer, and full for buttons, chips, tracks, and send. Controls are round: Button 28/30/32/36/40, IconButton 24/28/30/32, rows 34, chips 22, send 32.

Persistent surfaces have no borders and no shadows. Floating menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts, and dialogs have none either; their edge is the fill step. The only shadow left is `--shadow-overlay`, on the sidebar when it floats over the page at 900px and narrower.

The optional **Transparent surroundings** setting uses native blur on supported systems. Its **Transparency** slider ranges from 0% (solid palette tint) to 100% (native blur without a tint), defaulting to 35%. `.app-shell` paints the tint once behind the sidebar and window chrome through `--shell-opacity`; work planes, tool content, and expanded panels stay opaque. The saved level survives toggling transparency off and on. Keep `--bg` a solid palette color so gradients, previews, and custom colors keep working. System Reduce Transparency wins over the saved preference.

## Layout and controls

Toolbars are `max(44px, 32px / zoom)` and line up with the traffic lights. 52px headers appear only inside cards and sheets. The sidebar defaults to 272px (224 to 380) and respects saved widths; it paints nothing, and a full-height hairline in `--line` separates it from the work column. Its header leads with the OpenOrc wordmark (a 16px cube and the name at 15px in Immaculate Gothic), after the traffic lights where the OS draws them, then Inbox and the sidebar toggle; short of room the name gives way whole and the cube stays. The list head holds Search, its glyph in the status column and its word where titles start, which opens the palette and is the app's only search, then at the row's end the filter and New thread. A rail of round buttons runs beside the list: New thread, then Tasks, Pull requests, Schedules, and Orclings, Projects after a short divider, and at its foot the weekly usage, the update, and Settings (Memory lives in the right panel and the palette). The usage button is Settings' Usage bars, lit in ink one per quarter of the week left over the rest on a track and amber at 10% or less; the popup's table and the Settings cards keep their rings. It reports on the open thread's account, or anywhere else on the agent and account the new-thread composer has chosen, and opens a table of every account's 5-hour and weekly limits. On a thread whose account has no weekly limit, such as OpenCode's, the bars show plain, like the rail's other buttons, once its agent reports what the conversation cost, and open with that cost above the table; the rail never shows the figure itself. Claude Code prices turns even on a plan, so its figure is never shown. There are no Back/Forward buttons; their shortcuts still work. When navigation is hidden, the main toolbar keeps the window corner controls.

Threads is one list across every project, newest work first: Pinned leads, then Recent (the last three hours) and Today open and Yesterday folded, with Resolved at the foot (threads put there or quiet since before yesterday; drop a thread on it to resolve, drag one out to reopen); a folded day still shows the open thread and the threads that need you (unread, waiting on you, or with an agent on them, read apart so one past the loaded pages still shows), and search reaches the rest. Snoozed threads keep their own collapsible section. Thread rows show the muted title, then the project's icon and name with its pull request at the end; what the thread needs (working, needs you, failed, or unread) ends the line under the title, in one column with a parent's chevron; the open row takes `--current-row-fill` with `--navigation-ink`, shared by every pane showing it. Branch and provider details stay in accessible labels and hover text. The pinned view, pagination, folded sections, and context menus remain. The new thread's question names its project, which opens the project list with Add project. Command-click or Control-click opens a split pane.

Repository headings offer **Remove project** through right-click or Shift+F10 on a focused heading. Removal only hides the repository from project lists and pickers: files, worktrees, history, running work, and schedules are retained. Importing it again restores the same project. Workspace cannot be removed.

Imported projects use this icon priority: explicit choice, repository image, detected framework, detected language, outline folder. Workspace retains its globe. Click a project icon to select a discovered image, choose a local file, refresh candidates, or return to the folder. Discovery is a bounded local scan on first display, with positive and negative results stored under the app's `project-icons` data folder. It does not poll, watch repositories, execute project configuration, or call a network or model service. Manual selections survive refreshes; Automatic resumes discovery. Raster previews are at most 64px; small static SVGs stay vector images.

Framework and language fallbacks are 24 upstream Devicon brand SVGs shared through `ProjectStackIcon`: React, Next.js, NestJS, Vue, Nuxt, Svelte, Astro, Angular, Express, Laravel, Django, FastAPI, JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Go, Rust, Java, C#, Ruby, PHP, Swift, C, and C++. These are an explicit exception to the outline icon policy. Preserve the upstream shapes and colors; monochrome Express and Rust invert for dark appearance, while Astro and Django keep their colors on a light ground. Detection reads at most 256 root entries and five metadata files (64KB each); it does not walk source trees or dependency directories. It prefers specific frameworks over their dependencies and leaves workspace roots or ambiguous stacks on the folder fallback. Version-3 caches upgrade older automatic results once and preserve explicit choices.

Conversations use a 800px column with 32px reading gutters and 24px composer gutters, both 12px at 520px and in narrow panes. Prompts are borderless r18 bubbles aligned right; replies are unboxed ink prose under a turn head, with the work folded behind a receipt. The composer is one r20 surface on `--composer-input`: attach, destination, model, and mode on the left, activity, the context ring, and the 32px send pill on the right. The mode pill grows a small menu of levels; it never steps. Below, one footer holds the working folder on the left and the branch and Commit chips on the right; long paths and branches truncate before actions. Clicking the context ring opens details; compaction needs its explicit action. An empty running composer shows Stop; text or attachments restore Send. New thread keeps the tile ground with a white r24 composer under a 28/34 question (the Commit chip just over the composer, on its left), starter pills for an empty project draft, and recent rows below. Task creation and starting execution are separate actions, drafts survive navigation, and save states reflect acknowledged persistence.

The work panel is one opaque plane from the window's top edge to its bottom, its header and page on the same surface. Its tabs are plain labels at natural width that scroll sideways when they no longer fit, and keep their full names; a tab's glyph turns into its close button on hover. Changes is always the first tab, and the plus opens the New tab page. Tool pages start with a 48px bar holding their one ink pill. Web pages keep their own Back/Forward and address pill above an r14 viewport; the agent controls the selected website tab. Files opens on a lazily loaded folder tree with keyboard navigation and remembered expansion; below 580px of file-view width the explorer starts collapsed and opens as an overlay. Explorer dims Git-ignored files and `node_modules` and never lists `.git` or `.DS_Store`. Closing the panel preserves its tabs and running shells. At work-row widths of 788px or less, an open panel overlays the work area.

Tasks, Pull requests, Schedules, and Memory share one header: the page name, a round search that grows into a field, a filter menu, and one lg secondary create pill. Content follows in a 1120px column under quiet group headings. Settings is a flush plane sheet beside the sidebar, which lists its sections; cards on it are tile cards with hairlines between rows. Use text buttons for form submissions, empty states, decisions that need an explicit label, and a page's one create action; routine row and toolbar actions are round icon buttons with accessible names and tooltips.

The UI shows no keyboard shortcuts and never advertises capabilities. Text that was the only explanation of a control moves to `aria-description`. Reasons appear only on disabled controls, on demand.

Settings → Usage expands to a 1120px content column for Cost, Tokens, and Limits. Date ranges cover the past 24 hours or 7, 30, and 90 calendar days; an agent filter includes historical agents. A total and agent list sit beside a trend chart, stacking below 760px of content width. Token totals and a model/day table follow. The chart supports pointer inspection and arrow keys. Costs are API estimates, including on subscription plans. Codex uses recorded token deltas and published model prices, with cached-input discounts and recorded Fast mode; other agents supply their own estimates. Unknown model prices stay unavailable. Limits keeps the existing account cards and reset controls; provider recovery links open that view directly.

Agent updates live in Settings → Connections. Enabled agents share one group with hairlines between rows: mark, name, connection status, version, and enable switch stay visible; expanding a name reveals installation details. Add agents is a compact disclosure at the group's foot. Explicit Update and Update all actions, failures, and newer versions remain visible, with a manual check above and automatic-check preferences in a separate group below. A dismissible toast in the upper-right workspace leads to Connections through Review updates. OpenOrc release updates share that stack above agent updates, with Download update, Later, and Restart to update, a 3px progress track with a percentage, and readable failures. Later is remembered per version across windows and restarts. The stack covers the native Preview while visible; downloads and failed installs have Hide to restore it, and a hidden download returns when ready.

Claude accounts in Connections also share one group of rows. Names and identities lead, the default account has a quiet label, and connection states and overflow menus align at the end. Sign in and Cancel sign-in stay visible when needed; Rename, Make default, Sign in again, and Enable or Disable are menu actions. Rename and Add account expand inline forms, and account-switching preferences sit below the group.

Typing `/` opens commands and skills for the selected agent; Codex also accepts `$` for skills. Selection inserts the agent's native skill prefix. Suggestions float below New thread composers and above conversation composers, keep keyboard selection visible without moving focus out of the textarea, and dismiss on blur or an outside click without changing the draft. A skill named in the message is marked in `--mention-ink` and semibold, the same blue in every palette. The composer's highlight layer draws the letters and the textarea keeps the caret and selection, so a mark may color text but never change its width: the semibold is a text stroke around the regular letters, never a bolder face.

Responsive behavior follows available width. At 900px and narrower, the sidebar floats over the page and dismisses when a destination opens. Toolbars and properties wrap; secondary labels may yield while accessible names remain. Check narrow windows, split conversations, enlarged text, keyboard focus, and reduced motion.

## Icons and motion

The [Precision Outline family](precision-outline-icons.md) uses original geometry on a 24-unit grid, 1.75-unit strokes, rounded caps and joins, and `currentColor`, all from `components/icons.tsx`. Reuse the shared components rather than adding another icon library. Filled collaboration marks and the Hollow conversation indicator are deliberate exceptions.

Generic agent presence uses the single-color OpenOrc cube. Individual Orcling artwork keeps its shapes, colors, and personalization. Threads and Orclings share the sidebar and keep their last selected conversations. Companion home chats stay out of project thread lists, and private instructions and memory stay separate from Workspace and project memory.

Motion reads tokens only: leaving 160ms, arriving 220ms with opacity, a 4px blur, and a 4px rise, a 40ms stagger capped near eight rows, one overshoot for confirmations (260 to 360ms), and 420ms for shape resizes. Menus open out of the control that was touched and states swap in place. History loads and remounts never animate. Under reduced motion every duration and delay is zero and every keyframe ends visible. Status meaning must stay clear through text and shape as well as color.
