# Interface conventions

OpenOrc uses one tile ground for the sidebar and conversation, white planes for the work that needs its own room, and fill steps instead of borders and shadows. Shared components and semantic tokens keep conversations, task documents, and review panels consistent. The app-scoped [design record](../apps/desktop/DESIGN.md) lists the tokens and component recipes.

## Tokens and appearance

Color palettes are defined in `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/lib/theme-palettes.ts` and applied by `lib/theme.ts`. Components read semantic roles from `app.css`, never palette literals: `--bg` is the tile under the sidebar, the conversation, and page toolbars; `--surface` is the plane under the work panel, sheets, transcript cards, and inputs (darker than the tile in dark mode); `--surface-2` is the fill (hover, chips, tracks, fields); `--surface-3` is the strong fill (press, hover on a filled control, off tracks, meters); `--line` divides rows at `--hairline` width. `--ink` through `--ink-4` carry hierarchy; every role holds 4.5:1 on tile, plane, and fill, and faint text never sits on the strong fill. `--accent` and `--accent-fg` make the one ink pill per screen. `--ok`/`--bad` mark diffs and results, `--attention` colors dots, `--warn` is warning text.

Two contextual roles follow the ground they sit on. `--current-row-fill` (selected rows) and `--control-fill` (secondary pills and chips) are `--navigation-soft` and `--surface` on the tile; every plane restates them as `--surface-2`, and a tile card inside a plane restates them back to `--surface`. Mark a new plane with `data-plane` rather than adding it to the selector lists. Floating layers paint `--float-fill`: the plane over the tile and the tile over a plane, read under the placed layer by `lib/use-anchor-ground.ts`.

OpenOrc has its own tile, plane, and translucent ink fills; Cursor keeps its own grays; flat light palettes have a hand-tuned ground step so planes separate. Fill and line roles accept `#rrggbbaa`. Product presets keep their source colors. No palette paints a gradient: the sidebar, the work column and the panel share one ground color. [Theme references](theme-references.md) record the evidence and adaptations. Light, dark, and system appearance are independent of palette selection. Verify both modes, a second palette, and saved custom colors when changing a component.

Appearance separates action, navigation, and content accents. Use `--accent` and its companions for the primary action, `--navigation-ink`/`--navigation-soft` for selected destinations and rows, and `--content-ink`/`--content-soft` for links and inline code. Each palette defines its own defaults: Claude uses clay actions and blue content accents; GitHub uses green primary actions and blue links, following [Primer's color roles](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/); Eliron keeps graphite controls, pink navigation, and blue links. Accent combinations and custom role colors are stored per palette and mode. Editing or resetting Actions leaves Content intact; resetting all accents preserves unrelated overrides.

Typography, radii, control heights, and motion are `@theme static` tokens in `app.css`. The interface uses one regular weight; bold appears only for author emphasis inside replies, documents, and comments. UI text is 12, 13, or 14px, with 16, 22, and 28px headings and tabular 18/22 and 16/20 numerals. The interface font is an Appearance setting (Immaculate Gothic by default, Inter, Geist, or the system font), mapped in `fonts.css`. Code has its own setting (JetBrains Mono, Geist Mono, or System monospace) applied through `--font-mono` to code blocks, editors, diffs, and open terminals; terminals refit after the font loads without restarting their shell.

Corner radii are chosen per component: 10 for rows and menu items, 16 for drop menus and code blocks, 18 for popovers and prompt bubbles, 20 for other sheets, dialogs, and the composer, 24 for the New thread composer, and full for buttons, chips, tracks, and send. Controls are round: Button 28/30/32/36/40, IconButton 24/28/30/32, rows 34, chips 22, send 32.

Persistent surfaces have no borders and no shadows. Floating menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts, and dialogs have none either; their edge is the fill step. A layer that lies across the tile and a plane (a composer picker, an update toast over the transcript) draws its edge with a hairline ring instead. The only shadow left is `--shadow-overlay`, on the sidebar when it floats over the page at 900px and narrower. A select's list on Windows and Linux is the one exception to all of this: the page draws it in place of the native popup and dresses it as the macOS menu, with `--surface` at 85% behind a blur, a hairline edge, `--shadow-overlay`, and the accent on the row under the pointer or the keyboard.

The optional **Transparent surroundings** setting uses native blur on supported systems. Its **Transparency** slider ranges from 0% (solid palette tint) to 100% (native blur without a tint), defaulting to 35%. `.app-shell` paints the tint once behind the sidebar and window chrome through `--shell-opacity`; work planes, tool content, and expanded panels stay opaque. The saved level survives toggling transparency off and on. Keep `--bg` a solid palette color so gradients, previews, and custom colors keep working. System Reduce Transparency wins over the saved preference.

## Layout and controls

Toolbars are `max(44px, 32px / zoom)` and line up with the traffic lights. 52px headers appear only inside cards and sheets. The sidebar defaults to 272px (224 to 380) and respects saved widths; it paints nothing, and a full-height hairline in `--line` separates it from the work column. Its header leads with the OpenOrc wordmark (a 16px cube and the name at 15px in Immaculate Gothic), after the traffic lights where the OS draws them, then Back, Forward and the sidebar toggle; short of room the name gives way whole and the cube stays. The list head holds Search, its glyph in the status column and its word where titles start, which opens the palette and is the app's only search, then at the row's end the filter and New thread. A rail of round buttons runs beside the list: New thread, then Tasks, Pull requests, Schedules, and Orclings, Projects after a short divider, Inbox with its count after another, and at its foot the weekly usage, the update, and Settings (Memory lives in the right panel and the palette). The usage button is Settings' Usage bars, lit in ink one per quarter of the week left over the rest on a track and amber at 10% or less; the popup's table and the Settings cards keep their rings. It reports on the open thread's account, or anywhere else on the agent and account the new-thread composer has chosen, and opens a table of every account's 5-hour and weekly limits. On a thread whose account has no weekly limit, such as OpenCode's, the bars show plain, like the rail's other buttons, once its agent reports what the conversation cost, and open with that cost above the table; the rail never shows the figure itself. Claude Code prices turns even on a plan, so its figure is never shown. Back and Forward step through the window's history, as ⌘[ and ⌘] do. When navigation is hidden, the main toolbar keeps the window corner controls.

Threads is one list across every project, newest work first: Pinned leads, then Recent (the last three hours) and Today open and Yesterday folded, with Resolved at the foot (threads put there or quiet since before yesterday; drop a thread on it to resolve, drag one out to reopen); a folded day still shows the open thread and the threads that need you (unread, waiting on you, or with an agent on them, read apart so one past the loaded pages still shows), and search reaches the rest. Snoozed threads keep their own collapsible section. Thread rows show the muted title, then the project's icon and name with its pull request at the end; what the thread needs (working, needs you, failed, or unread) ends the line under the title, in one column with a parent's chevron; the open row takes `--current-row-fill` with `--navigation-ink`, shared by every pane showing it. Branch and provider details stay in accessible labels and hover text. The pinned view, pagination, folded sections, and context menus remain. The new thread's question names its project, which opens the project list with Add project. Command-click or Control-click opens a split pane.

Repository headings offer **Remove project** through right-click or Shift+F10 on a focused heading. Removal only hides the repository from project lists and pickers: files, worktrees, history, running work, and schedules are retained. Importing it again restores the same project. Workspace cannot be removed.

Imported projects use this icon priority: explicit choice, repository image, detected framework, detected language, outline folder. Workspace retains its globe. Click a project icon to select a discovered image, choose a local file, refresh candidates, or return to the folder. Discovery is a bounded local scan on first display, with positive and negative results stored under the app's `project-icons` data folder. It does not poll, watch repositories, execute project configuration, or call a network or model service. Manual selections survive refreshes; Automatic resumes discovery. Raster previews are at most 64px; small static SVGs stay vector images.

Framework and language fallbacks are 24 upstream Devicon brand SVGs shared through `ProjectStackIcon`: React, Next.js, NestJS, Vue, Nuxt, Svelte, Astro, Angular, Express, Laravel, Django, FastAPI, JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Go, Rust, Java, C#, Ruby, PHP, Swift, C, and C++. These are an explicit exception to the outline icon policy. Preserve the upstream shapes and colors; monochrome Express and Rust invert for dark appearance, while Astro and Django keep their colors on a light ground. Detection reads at most 256 root entries and five metadata files (64KB each); it does not walk source trees or dependency directories. It prefers specific frameworks over their dependencies and leaves workspace roots or ambiguous stacks on the folder fallback. Version-3 caches upgrade older automatic results once and preserve explicit choices.

Conversations use a 800px column with 32px reading gutters and 24px composer gutters, both 12px at 520px and in narrow panes. Prompts are borderless r18 bubbles aligned right; replies are unboxed ink prose under a turn head, with the work folded behind a receipt. The composer is one r20 surface on `--composer-input`: attach, destination, model, and mode on the left, activity, the context ring, and the 32px send pill on the right. The mode pill grows a small menu of levels; it never steps. When there are uncommitted changes, the Commit chip leads the composer stack at its left edge. Queued messages, background work, cloud status, recovery, and all other notices follow it, before the writing surface. Clicking the context ring opens details; compaction needs its explicit action. An empty running composer shows Stop; text or attachments restore Send. New thread keeps the tile ground with a white r24 composer under a 28/34 question (the Commit chip just over the composer, on its left) and the location row under it; along the bottom, a quiet halftone of the project's year of turns, one dot a day, under a line of its turns and steps since the first active day, with the day's detail on hover. New subthread shares it. The column centres as if the activity were not there. Task creation and starting execution are separate actions, drafts survive navigation, and save states reflect acknowledged persistence.

On phones, New thread places the environment and project selectors side by side above the composer. The project selector opens a searchable vertical sheet with Workspace and the imported projects; model and mode stay inside the composer.

On phones paired with a Mac that lists Orclings, Home floats a bottom bar 62 pt tall: its tabs as glyphs on one Liquid Glass capsule centered on the screen, each tab as wide as fits (52 to 74 pt) with at least 24 pt between them and New, and New at the trailing edge as a round button of the same untinted glass, its plus in ink. New starts what the tab in front makes: a thread on Threads; on Orclings, a native menu that opens from the button with New Orcling and New Thread, where the Mac lets a phone make Orclings, otherwise a thread; on Tasks, the New task screen. New task reads as the task screen does: the title field at the head, then Backlog, a priority menu and a project menu as pills, then the description as Markdown text, with Create task as the ink pill above the keyboard. It saves in a worktree when the Mac's default is one and the project can branch, otherwise in the checkout, and opens the saved task in its place; the draft stays on the phone until then. The chosen tab sits on a quiet ink platter; a touch raises a clear glass lens from it, 30% larger so it rises over the bar's edges, which follows the finger across the tabs with a selection tick at each and chooses the tab it is let go over. The glyph under the lens is drawn above it, grown and in ink. VoiceOver chooses a tab with a double tap, and under Reduce Motion the lens moves without springs. Threads keeps its list under a row of Orcling faces, the first face's body on the wordmark cube's edge, each with an `--attention` dot cut out of the ground at its top right (`--bad` after a failed turn) while its conversation waits on the person or has texts unread; the row has its own switch under This iPhone, on by default, and while it is off the Orclings tab carries a neutral count of the Orclings that need you. Orclings lists each by face, name, newest message and time, newest first, as a messaging app does: the name in ink at 18 pt, the newest message on one faint 16 pt line read as plain text, the time faint at the end of the name's line, an unread dot at the message line's end and a quiet Working tag after the name. Each face moves, built from the flat drawing in layers: it breathes at rest, hops with squash and stretch while its Orcling works, and nudges side to side while it wants the person; its eyes blink and glance on a pace of their own, and glasses follow the glance without blinking. Faces hold still while another tab or a pushed screen covers the list, and under Reduce Motion they are the still drawing. An Orcling's own conversation opens in the chat layout above with the thread's composer, and holding a bubble offers the six tapbacks. Paired with an older Mac, Home keeps the New thread pill and shows no Orclings. Creating and editing Orclings stays on the Mac.

On phones, Home's Threads, Orclings and Tasks lists share one hierarchy, from ink and size alone: titles and names in full ink at 18 pt on one line (a sub thread's at 16 pt), and the line under them faint, so the light theme's close muted and faint inks never carry it; a working thread's title shimmers with a faint band across the ink. The Tasks tab reads as the desktop task list on Home's grid: status groups in the desktop's order, each folding under a Home-style heading with its count; each row has the desktop's status mark in the lead column, the title over its project and age, and its priority bars in the action column, with unlit bars drawn faint behind them. Holding a row offers its thread and its title to copy. A task opens as its own screen with no bar title until its page title scrolls under the bar; then the bar shows the title over its status and project, as a thread's does. The page title edits in place and saves when it loses focus. The desktop's property pills follow: status and priority open native menus, the project is read-only, and labels edit in one comma-separated prompt. Then its branch, the conversation working on it and the one it came from, and the description under a Description heading whose Edit opens a full-height sheet of the Markdown text. A description that shows a redacted secret stays editable only on the Mac. A task an agent saves or starts, and a thread it starts, show in the transcript as r24 plane cards that open them; a Mac that cannot open tasks keeps the call's row. A Home row's long-press menu leads with New Subthread, as the desktop's sidebar menu does, and the open thread's More menu keeps it with the thread's family; it opens the New thread composer under a question naming the parent and a line with the parent's project and folder, starts on the parent's model, mode, permission and account, and works in the parent's folder. Team threads, threads in the cloud and Orcling conversations offer none, and an older Mac says it needs an update before anything is written. A thread's More menu lists its subthreads, with their count, and a subthread's menu leads back to its parent. Starting, moving and deleting tasks stay on the Mac.

The work panel is one opaque plane from the window's top edge to its bottom, its header and page on the same surface. Its tabs are plain labels at natural width that scroll sideways when they no longer fit, and keep their full names; a tab's glyph turns into its close button on hover. Changes is always the first tab, and the plus opens the New tab page. Tool pages start with a 48px bar holding their one ink pill. Web pages keep their own Back/Forward and address pill above an r14 viewport; the agent controls the selected website tab. Files opens on a lazily loaded folder tree with keyboard navigation and remembered expansion; below 580px of file-view width the explorer starts collapsed and opens as an overlay. Explorer dims Git-ignored files and `node_modules` and never lists `.git` or `.DS_Store`. Closing the panel preserves its tabs and running shells. At work-row widths of 788px or less, an open panel overlays the work area.

Tasks, Pull requests, Schedules, and Memory share one header: the page name, a round search that grows into a field, a filter menu, and one lg primary create pill. Content follows in a 1120px column under quiet group headings. Settings is a flush plane sheet beside the sidebar, which lists its sections; cards on it are tile cards with hairlines between rows. Use text buttons for form submissions, empty states, decisions that need an explicit label, and a page's one create action; routine row and toolbar actions are round icon buttons with accessible names and tooltips.

Task details open on Overview. Their tab track sits 24px below the top rail and shares the document's 800px column and 48px gutters, reducing to 24px below 600px and 16px below 400px. Overview, Activity, Commits, and Memory start 32px below the track in that column; Changes uses the workspace width. Each tab replaces the page body. Memory reads the task's saved history even before an execution conversation exists. Arrow keys, Home, and End move between tabs; leaving Overview waits for its edits to save.

The UI shows no keyboard shortcuts and never advertises capabilities. Text that was the only explanation of a control moves to `aria-description`. Reasons appear only on disabled controls, on demand.

Settings → Usage expands to a 1120px content column for Cost, Tokens, and Limits. Date ranges cover the past 24 hours or 7, 30, and 90 calendar days; an agent filter includes historical agents. A total and agent list sit beside a trend chart, stacking below 760px of content width. Token totals and a model/day table follow. The chart supports pointer inspection and arrow keys. Costs are API estimates, including on subscription plans. Codex uses recorded token deltas and published model prices, with cached-input discounts and recorded Fast mode; other agents supply their own estimates. Unknown model prices stay unavailable. Limits keeps the existing account cards and reset controls; provider recovery links open that view directly.

Agent updates live in Settings → Connections. Enabled agents share one group with hairlines between rows: mark, name, connection status, version, and enable switch stay visible; expanding a name reveals installation details. Add agents is a compact disclosure at the group's foot. Explicit Update and Update all actions, Stop on a row that is updating, failures, and newer versions remain visible, with a manual check above and automatic-check preferences in a separate group below. A dismissible toast in the upper-right workspace leads to Connections through Review updates. OpenOrc release updates share that stack above agent updates, with Download update, Later, and Restart to update, a 3px progress track with a percentage, and readable failures. Later is remembered per version across windows and restarts. The stack covers the native Preview while visible; downloads and failed installs have Hide to restore it, and a hidden download returns when ready.

Settings keeps Refresh connections beside Coding agents, Pair a phone beside Paired phones, and connector actions beside the page description above the connector list. The Add connector dialog uses sheet fields, a segmented Server URL/Command choice, and agent marks with checkboxes in two columns that collapse to one when space is tight. Headers and environment variables open on demand. Its fields scroll while the title, validation message, and action footer stay visible.

Claude accounts in Connections also share one group of rows. Names and identities lead, the default account has a quiet label, and connection states and overflow menus align at the end. Sign in and Cancel sign-in stay visible when needed; Rename, Make default, Sign in again, and Enable or Disable are menu actions. Rename and Add account expand inline forms, and account-switching preferences sit below the group.

Typing `/` opens commands and skills for the selected agent; Codex also accepts `$` for skills. Selection inserts the agent's native skill prefix. Suggestions float below New thread composers and above conversation composers, keep keyboard selection visible without moving focus out of the textarea, and dismiss on blur or an outside click without changing the draft. A skill named in the message is marked in `--mention-ink` and semibold, the same blue in every palette. The composer's highlight layer draws the letters and the textarea keeps the caret and selection, so a mark may color text but never change its width: the semibold is a text stroke around the regular letters, never a bolder face.

Responsive behavior follows available width. At 900px and narrower, the sidebar floats over the page and dismisses when a destination opens. Toolbars and properties wrap; secondary labels may yield while accessible names remain. Check narrow windows, split conversations, enlarged text, keyboard focus, and reduced motion.

## Icons and motion

First-run setup uses a split composition: agent, appearance, or project controls on the left and a decorative collage on the right. Above 900px, the controls align with the window wordmark and the artwork sits toward the right edge, with a flexible gap between them. The three-segment progress track, Back, and Continue sit in a fixed bottom bar outside the scroll area. Below 720px the artwork yields to the controls. Appearance starts with six palette choices and an expandable catalog that keeps the selected palette visible. The workspace preview renders the actual app shell and New thread screen at desktop dimensions, scaled down uniformly. Its example data and storage are isolated from setup, while the selected palette, mode, and saved custom colors update the preview; disposable setup previews do not save those choices. Flat original artwork keeps its own blue, orange, and green palette, while the interface and preview read semantic tokens. Tiles arrive with a short stagger and rearrange when the step changes; reduced motion shows the settled composition. Recovery keeps the compact single-column layout. First-run agent choices form two columns directly on the page, with a logo, stacked name and status, and checkbox; the whole ready row toggles selection. At 400px and narrower, the choices use one column. The default-agent selector follows the choices, so selection precedes the default choice. Setup actions remain visible, while installation metadata stays in Connections. The content supports wheel, trackpad, and keyboard scrolling without a visible scrollbar.

The [Precision Outline family](precision-outline-icons.md) uses original geometry on a 24-unit grid, 1.75-unit strokes, rounded caps and joins, and `currentColor`, all from `components/icons.tsx`. Reuse the shared components rather than adding another icon library. Filled collaboration marks and the Hollow conversation indicator are deliberate exceptions.

Generic agent presence uses the single-color OpenOrc cube. Individual Orcling artwork keeps its shapes, colors, and personalization. Threads and Orclings share the sidebar and keep their last selected conversations. Companion home chats stay out of project thread lists, and private instructions and memory stay separate from Workspace and project memory. An Orcling's own chat reads as texting: the person's messages keep the prompt bubble, the Orcling's sit beside its 28px face on a soft tint of its body color (14% light, 20% dark), one bubble per paragraph with facing corners closed to 6px within a run, three pulsing dots while it works, and tapbacks resting on a bubble's top corner inside a ring of the ground. Pointing at one of its bubbles shows a reaction button beside it, which opens a pill of six tapbacks above the bubble; choosing one closes it. In a group chat each Orcling's runs take its own tint, with its name above them in 12px ink-3; a message one Orcling sent another reads "Pip to Bo". A group shows as overlapping faces, cut out of the ground with a ring of it.

Motion reads tokens only: leaving 160ms, arriving 220ms with opacity, a 4px blur, and a 4px rise, a 40ms stagger capped near eight rows, one overshoot for confirmations (260 to 360ms), and 420ms for shape resizes. Menus open out of the control that was touched and states swap in place. History loads and remounts never animate. Under reduced motion every duration and delay is zero and every keyframe ends visible. Status meaning must stay clear through text and shape as well as color.
