# Interface conventions

OpenOrc uses a near-white ground for the conversation and panel, a faintly cool sidebar, soft grey fills, and white planes lifted by a faint edge for the work that needs its own room; no borders. Shared components and semantic tokens keep conversations, task documents, and review panels consistent. The app-scoped [design record](../apps/desktop/DESIGN.md) lists the tokens and component recipes.

## Tokens and appearance

Color palettes are defined in `packages/client/src/theme/theme-palettes.ts` and applied by `lib/theme.ts`. Components read semantic roles from `app.css`, never palette literals: `--bg` is the ground under the conversation, the panel and page toolbars; `--sidebar` is the sidebar's (the ground unless a palette tints it); `--well` is a piece on the ground (the composer, transcript cards, Settings' tiles; in light, the palette's plane; in dark, the ground carved more than halfway to black), `--well-ring` its edge (a thin ring and a faint shadow in light; in dark none, or a hairline where the ground is already near black) and `--key` a control inside a piece (the fill in light); `--surface` is the plane under code, diffs and floating layers, lifted by `--raised-shadow`; `--surface-2` is the fill (hover, chips, tracks, fields); `--surface-3` is the strong fill (press, hover on a filled control, off tracks, meters); `--line` divides rows at `--hairline` width. `--ink` through `--ink-4` carry hierarchy, with `--prose-ink` for reading text and `--line-number` for diffs; every text role, `--ok`, `--bad` and `--warn` included, holds 4.5:1 on the ground, the sidebar, the plane, the piece, the key, the fill and the selected and hovered rows; line numbers hold 3:1 on the ground, the plane and the diff's washes; and faint text never sits on the strong fill. `--accent` and `--accent-fg` make the one ink pill per screen. `--ok`/`--bad` mark diffs and results, `--attention` colors dots, `--warn` is warning text.

Two contextual roles follow the ground they sit on. `--current-row-fill` (selected rows) and `--control-fill` (secondary pills and chips) are `--navigation-soft` and `--surface` on the tile; every plane restates them as `--surface-2`, and a tile card inside a plane restates them back to `--surface`. Mark a new plane with `data-plane` rather than adding it to the selector lists. Floating layers paint `--float-fill`: the plane over the tile and the tile over a plane, read under the placed layer by `lib/use-anchor-ground.ts`.

OpenOrc has its own near-white ground, cool sidebar and grey fills; Cursor keeps its own grays; flat light palettes have a hand-tuned ground step so planes separate. Fill and line roles accept `#rrggbbaa`. Product presets keep their source colors. No palette paints a gradient, and only OpenOrc tints its sidebar apart from the ground. [Theme references](theme-references.md) record the evidence and adaptations. Light, dark, and system appearance are independent of palette selection. Verify both modes, a second palette, and saved custom colors when changing a component.

Appearance separates action, navigation, and content accents. Use `--accent` and its companions for the primary action, `--navigation-ink`/`--navigation-soft` for selected destinations and rows, and `--content-ink`/`--content-soft` for links and inline code. Each palette defines its own defaults: Claude uses clay actions and blue content accents; GitHub uses green primary actions and blue links, following [Primer's color roles](https://primer.style/product/getting-started/foundations/color-usage/); Eliron keeps graphite controls, pink navigation, and blue links. Accent combinations and custom role colors are stored per palette and mode. Editing or resetting Actions leaves Content intact; resetting all accents preserves unrelated overrides.

Typography, radii, control heights, and motion are `@theme static` tokens in `app.css`. The interface uses one regular weight; bold appears only for author emphasis inside replies, documents, and comments. UI text is 12.5, 13, 14 or 15px (quiet lines, labels, rows and reading text, a header's title), with 16, 22, and 28px headings and tabular 18/22 and 16/20 numerals. The interface font is an Appearance setting (Immaculate Gothic by default, Inter, Geist, or the system font), mapped in `fonts.css`. Code has its own setting (JetBrains Mono, Geist Mono, or System monospace) applied through `--font-mono` to code blocks, editors, diffs, and open terminals; terminals refit after the font loads without restarting their shell.

Corner radii are chosen per component: 10 for rows and menu items, 16 for drop menus and code blocks, 18 for popovers and prompt bubbles, 20 for other sheets, dialogs, and the composer, 24 for the New thread composer, and full for buttons, chips, tracks, and send. Controls are round: Button 28/30/32/36/40, IconButton 24/28/30/32, rows 34, chips 22, send 32.

Persistent surfaces have no borders. Pieces are set in with no ring and no shadow in light. Floating menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts, and dialogs wear `--raised-shadow`, a strong hairline and one soft shadow in OpenOrc and nothing in palettes whose fill step reads alone. A layer that lies across the tile and a plane (a composer picker, an update toast over the transcript) draws its edge with a hairline ring instead. `--shadow-overlay` is the floating sidebar's, at 900px and narrower.

The optional **Transparent surroundings** setting uses native blur on supported systems. Its **Transparency** slider ranges from 0% (solid palette tint) to 100% (native blur without a tint), defaulting to 35%. `.app-shell` paints the tint once behind the sidebar and window chrome through `--shell-opacity`; work planes, tool content, and expanded panels stay opaque. The saved level survives toggling transparency off and on. Keep `--bg` a solid palette color so gradients, previews, and custom colors keep working. System Reduce Transparency wins over the saved preference.

## Layout and controls

Toolbars are `max(44px, 32px / zoom)` and line up with the traffic lights. The sidebar is one column, 247px by default (224 to 380), on `--sidebar`, and a hairline in `--line` runs the window's full height at its right edge, through its header. Its header has no line under it and clears the traffic lights. The wordmark uses a 16px cube and the name at 15px in Immaculate Gothic. The sidebar toggle leads the first header beside the sidebar, open or hidden. The signed-in space switcher shows OpenOrc in Personal and the organization's mark and name in an organization. Back and Forward sit at the end of the sidebar header when they fit beside the whole name. If they do not fit, they follow the sidebar toggle in the work column's header, as they do while the sidebar is hidden, and return when room allows. One measurement chooses their location, so exactly one pair is shown, dimmed when its history is empty. The name is never cut to make room for the arrows; a name too long for the row alone keeps whole-word clipping and its full-name tooltip. Signed out, the existing plain wordmark and header behavior remain. In the work column's header the open threads are tabs in the order they were opened or dragged to, each its mark and name as plain words: the open one in full ink over a 2px line, the others in the third ink. Under the header New thread leads, a pen in full ink, lit while its screen is open; then the app's screens are named rows: Tasks, Pull requests, Schedules and Orclings, each with an amber count only where something waits on the person or failed; the sidebar shows no grey totals, and Resolved is a door with a chevron (Memory lives in the right panel and the palette). There is no Inbox: a thread waiting on an approval or a question shows it on its own row and its project's label, and tasks that wait (an approval, a proposal, a review) count on Tasks and lead its list. The threads follow under a quiet "Workspaces" head with Search, the app's only search, which opens the palette, Add project (a folder with a plus) and the filter, which also hides the workspaces the person unticks; Workspaces folds as a whole. Text without a lead starts 16px in, on the traffic lights' left edge; screen glyphs and project marks share one 18px lead slot there, so their names start in one column at 42, and counts and row marks centre 24px in from the sidebar's edge. A project's mark (its chosen or detected icon, a folder, the cloud for a project only OpenOrc Cloud has, No project's globe) leads its name wherever it is named: the sidebar's labels, the scope and its menu, the palette's project rows and Pull requests' groups. Threads are grouped by project under quiet labels, rows flush with them, the most recently active on top: the four latest and every other thread at work or waiting, before "Show N more", held in place while the pointer or the keyboard is in the list, and a line at the list's edge names what needs the person past it. The 44px footer, under a hairline, holds the person (signed in, their face, name and plan; signed out, Sign in, which opens OpenOrc's sign-in page in the browser), the weekly usage, the update while one waits, and Settings. The person has a 36px row with a 26px face, a 14/16 name and a 12/14 plan line; the tools keep their centre line and the gear stays 24px in from the edge. The usage button is the week's share left filled on a 16px disc of its own ink at 20%, never an arc, then the number, in a 28px pill, both amber at 10% or less; the popup's table draws its shares the same way, and the Settings cards keep their rings. It reports on the open thread's account, or anywhere else on the agent and account the new-thread composer has chosen, and opens a table of every account's 5-hour and weekly limits. On a thread whose account has no weekly limit, such as OpenCode's, its glyph shows plain, like the footer's other buttons, once its agent reports what the conversation cost, and open with that cost above the table; the footer never shows the figure itself. Claude Code prices turns even on a plan, so its figure is never shown.

Scoped views keep project groups, counts, search and task lists within the selected space. An organization contains the local folders paired with its usable cloud projects and its readable cloud threads; Personal keeps unmatched folders and personal cloud work. A cloud project without a local folder uses the cloud mark and offers OpenOrc Cloud only. The organization filter adds Mine and Everyone's above the existing thread filters, without a heading. A window retains each space's tabs, split panes, destination and Back history independently. The account footer remains personal. Orclings and their group chats stay global, with their existing list and amber count in every space. Switching spaces from them, as from Settings, restores that space's last project-work destination. Tasks, Pull requests and Schedules follow the projects the current space lists, with amber counts using the same scope.

Create organization and Edit organization share a 420px dialog with the built Close control, an optional 48px raster logo, Name, Cancel and one primary action. Name refusals stay beside the input. A failed logo save after creation retains the created organization and offers Try again on its empty screen. The empty organization names itself above Add projects from GitHub and Invite people. The sidebar says “No projects yet.” for its admin, or “No projects available” for a member with none, including on People and Tasks. Add projects uses a 460px dialog with a searchable repository list and individual results. Its quiet “Choose repositories on GitHub” action opens a menu of connected accounts followed by “Another account or organization”; without connected accounts it starts connecting directly. An account opens its own GitHub settings page, without a return flow. An unavailable page leaves that account listed and disabled, with “Can't open this account on GitHub right now.” on demand. The list rereads on window focus and each opening, keeping selections and successful additions. A refused default GitHub connection keeps its words beside the action and adds the separate quiet action “Another account or organization”. The original action keeps its meaning; nothing retries or starts another flow by itself. With nothing selected its disabled key reads “Add projects”; after selection it reads “Add 1 project” or the plural count. Only the selected organization's installations and repositories appear; adding a cloud project never invents a local folder.

Settings > Account leads with the person's 44px face, name and address, with Edit and Sign out aligned to the account tile. Edit profile uses the same 420px identity form as an organization: a 48px face, Change picture, Remove, Name, Cancel and Save changes. An unchanged or empty name disables Save. Pictures are cropped locally to 128px and saved as normalized WebP; the shared face uses initials on a neutral fill when no picture is set. A successful save uses the stored identity in its reply, so later read failures never offer that write again. Other devices, People, teammate starters and invitation details update live. The Personal menu row keeps the OpenOrc mark, matching Personal at rest; invitation detail keeps the organization's mark with a small face beside the inviter's name.

People uses the shared list-page column with aligned Name, Plan and Role columns. Members see the same Pro facts as admins. The sole admin's own role is plain Admin; with another admin present, it becomes the normal role picker. A successful self-demotion immediately removes admin controls. More holds Edit organization, Leave organization and Delete organization with the appropriate permissions. Pending invitations are absent when there are none. Leaving or deleting restores Personal; removing someone keeps People open.

Invite people uses a person with a plus in the switcher and palette; Create organization keeps the plain plus. Invite people uses one 480px batch form, one role and a result for each address. Invalid addresses stay beside their errors; duplicates are sent once. Delivery failures keep a copyable link. A refusal keeps the form and the server’s words, with an enabled Try again that sends only refused addresses. Successful results stay visible and are never sent twice on retry. Editing the addresses begins a new send and restores Send invitations. Closing stops further addresses without undoing admitted invitations. The admin’s Invitations section follows People only when rows exist; Copy link uses the server’s URL, Resend counts down the server’s remaining delay with its reason on hover, and Revoke asks for confirmation. Logged mail says Not emailed. View invitations opens People at that section.

Received invitations appear only for their signed-in address and expire from the switcher at their deadline. Opening an invitation name shows its detail without joining; Join selects the organization and Decline leaves the current work alone. Web acceptance only updates the list and never moves another window.

If an organization write succeeds before its following read fails, say that the write worked and keep the read's own refusal. Retry reads only and must never repeat that completed write. Create and Join keep their detail with “You created [organization].” or “You joined [organization].” first, then the read error, and Open [organization]. Creation's name and logo become read-only; Join no longer offers Decline. A later good read completes the requested opening only while that detail remains open; Close cancels navigation, leaving the organization or membership intact and allowing the switcher to update quietly.

When the first spaces read fails without a cached index, the signed-in OpenOrc switcher remains. Its menu shows Personal checked, one quiet line with the read's own refusal and Try again beside it, then Create organization… disabled with that reason on demand. No failure dot or colour appears on the wordmark. The periodic reread can heal it; current work stays in place.

Project access opens from the project menu for organization admins. Its 520px dialog offers Everyone or Only people you choose; admins stay checked and cannot be unchecked. Chosen people remain remembered when Everyone is selected. A member with no projects sees the organization, “An admin needs to give you access to a project.” and People, with no Add project control.

When project access goes, its group and rows leave under the sidebar’s hold-still rule. An open cloud tab retains its title and position; the conversation and composer give way to the same quiet organization state, saying “You no longer have access to this project.” with People. Access returning restores the conversation in that tab. Project-scoped Tasks and Pull requests fall back to the space’s all-projects scope without a notice.

A change made elsewhere changes the project lists without moving an open local tab, its running turn or the person. This works in both directions: a folder returning to Personal after lost access, or leaving Personal after joining, a project addition or a grant. The open local conversation stays fully usable until close or space switch; New thread from that retained folder offers only this Mac's places until its listed space is selected.

Workspaces groups threads by project, with Pinned before the projects and Snoozed after them. Project groups keep their order while the most recently active threads lead within each; they show four recent threads and all nonquiet threads before Show N more. Resolved stays at the foot for threads put there or quiet since before yesterday, with drag to resolve or reopen. Existing folding, pagination and selection rules remain.

Thread rows are one line: 32px high with 2px between rows, including children, on the named navigation rows' 34px pitch. They sit 8px from each sidebar edge with 8px horizontal padding, a 10px radius and a 14px regular title on an 18px line. Root titles start at x16; children step 24px to the existing depth cap. Quiet, working and background-command titles use `--ink-2`; waiting, failed and unread titles use `--ink`; hover uses `--ink`; an open pane uses `--navigation-ink` on `--current-row-fill`. Hover keeps `--navigation-hover`.

End marks keep the order teammate face, pull request, cloud and state, each 16px with 4px gaps and 8px before the strip. Only present marks take space, packed against the right edge. The last present mark is centred at x223 in a 247px sidebar and x200 at 224px, aligned with the navigation counts. Four present marks have centres 163/183/203/223 and 140/160/180/200 respectively. A lone PR occupies the last position; a plain row reserves no strip. The title uses all space up to the first present mark. Marks shift when other marks appear or disappear, without an added movement animation; the title start and row height stay fixed. The face is 14px with an initials fallback, PR and cloud glyphs are 14px, the working cube is 16px, question/failure/command marks are 14px and the unread dot is 7px. State precedence, the shared cube clock and its open-row ink stay unchanged. Teammate asks use the quiet question; the person's own asks use attention ink. Open PRs use the third ink, merged PRs `--ok`, closed PRs `--bad`, with their distinct glyphs. Every cloud thread has its cloud, including cloud-only projects, dimmed while its host is unreachable. Local checkouts and worktrees have no place mark. The title truncates first. Only at deep indents where even an ellipsis would not fit, remove outer columns in order: face, PR, cloud. State and disclosure remain. A parent's 24px disclosure stays last, with its present marks packed before it, ending 24px left of the usual line. Children keep their own state; the parent's aggregate still uses `threadTreeStatus`. Branch lines step 24px and stop 4px earlier to clear child titles.

Hover and keyboard focus show one metadata line, separated by `·`: latest activity age, project outside its project group, teammate's full name, place, branch or folder, PR number and state. Age uses the existing `now`, `3m`, `1h` vocabulary. Place is OpenOrc Cloud, Your own cloud, Worktree or This Mac. Include the actual branch, including main; without a branch, use the folder when available. Omit missing facts. Pinned, Snoozed and Resolved use the same row, with the project immediately after age in the tooltip. A clipped title appears complete above the facts; an uncut title is not repeated. Row facts use the existing 13/16 tooltip, 5px vertical and 9px horizontal padding, 10px radius, 6px offset and 800ms hover delay, widening to the viewport before wrapping. Descriptions include these facts and the teammate; status, cloud and PR keep descriptive accessible names. Subagents retain their completion/failure marks and spoken type, with the existing ticking elapsed time as a final `Running for …` fact only while running. Sorting, grouping, pagination, folding, split selection, drag and drop, keyboard movement, right-click menus and row destinations stay unchanged. There is no selected-row More control.

The new thread's question names its project, which opens the project list with Add project. Command-click or Control-click opens a split pane.

A project's label in the sidebar offers its actions (New thread, New task, Choose icon, Project settings, **Remove project**) from a More button that shows on hover or keyboard focus, and on right-click; a project picker's heading offers Remove project through right-click or Shift+F10. Removal only hides the repository from project lists and pickers: files, worktrees, history, running work, and schedules are retained. Importing it again restores the same project. Workspace, listed in the sidebar and pickers as No project, cannot be removed.

Imported projects use this icon priority: explicit choice, repository image, detected framework, detected language, outline folder. Workspace retains its globe. Click a project icon to select a discovered image, choose a local file, refresh candidates, or return to the folder. Discovery is a bounded local scan on first display, with positive and negative results stored under the app's `project-icons` data folder. It does not poll, watch repositories, execute project configuration, or call a network or model service. Manual selections survive refreshes; Automatic resumes discovery. Raster previews are at most 64px; small static SVGs stay vector images.

Framework and language fallbacks are 24 upstream Devicon brand SVGs shared through `ProjectStackIcon`: React, Next.js, NestJS, Vue, Nuxt, Svelte, Astro, Angular, Express, Laravel, Django, FastAPI, JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Go, Rust, Java, C#, Ruby, PHP, Swift, C, and C++. These are an explicit exception to the outline icon policy. Preserve the upstream shapes and colors; monochrome Express and Rust invert for dark appearance, while Astro and Django keep their colors on a light ground. Detection reads at most 256 root entries and five metadata files (64KB each); it does not walk source trees or dependency directories. It prefers specific frameworks over their dependencies and leaves workspace roots or ambiguous stacks on the folder fallback. Version-3 caches upgrade older automatic results once and preserve explicit choices.

Conversations use a 800px column with 32px reading gutters and 24px composer gutters, both 12px at 520px and in narrow panes. Prompts are borderless r18 bubbles on the fill aligned right; replies are unboxed prose in `--prose-ink` with ink headings and bold leads under a turn head, with the work folded behind a receipt. The composer is one r20 surface on `--composer-input`: attach, destination, model, and mode on the left, activity, the context ring, and the 32px send pill on the right. The mode pill grows a small menu of levels; it never steps. When there are uncommitted changes, the Commit chip leads the composer stack at its left edge. Queued messages, background work, cloud status, recovery, and all other notices follow it, before the writing surface. Clicking the context ring opens details; compaction needs its explicit action. An empty running composer shows Stop; text or attachments restore Send. An approval or a question the agent waits on docks at the top of the composer's piece above a hairline, not in the thread: its top line ends in Decline or Deny and says how many more wait, a question's choices are keys (one choice answers when pressed), an approval offers a line to say what to do instead (sent as the person's next message, for every agent) and Allow once as the column's one ink. Words in the field stay a message; the keyboard enters an ask only at its question, so a stray Enter answers nothing. Answered, it becomes the thread's record: a question card with its picks as keys with an ink edge, or one line for a denial. New thread keeps the ground with a white r24 composer under a 28/34 question (the Commit chip just over the composer, on its left) and the location row under it; along the bottom, a quiet halftone of the project's year of turns, one dot a day, under a line of its turns and steps since the first active day, with the day's detail on hover. New subthread shares it. The column centres as if the activity were not there. Task creation and starting execution are separate actions, drafts survive navigation, and save states reflect acknowledged persistence.

A live turn shows its head and timer as soon as it starts. An ask waiting in the composer does not count as turn content; once its turn rests, an ask alone leaves no head or empty space in the conversation. Prompts and answered records stay visible. Views without a dock keep asks in the conversation. The last visible turn keeps Copy and Fork at rest; a turn held only in the composer does not hide that row.

Threads on this Mac and on OpenOrc Cloud show the first nonblank line of the first message until the person or the small model names them. The first line is shortened to 72 characters after secrets are masked. After the first exchange, the sender’s app uses the conversation’s agent to propose a short name, only while the current name still matches that first line. A rename during generation wins, and another device cannot replace a name already saved. Teammates see the saved name live in the row and tab. An empty conversation reads “New thread”; a row or tab never has a blank name.

A teammate's thread that the person can only read keeps the field editable, with a quiet placeholder naming whose thread it is. One read-only rule disables Send, Queue, attach, the model and the mode, with the same reason on demand; Enter cannot send and Stop is absent. The pickers still show what the thread runs on. Its send cluster is always one bare disabled arrow, whether the thread works or rests and whether the draft is empty or has words. An owner's thread out of reach blocks sending but keeps its local choices. Drafts stay on this device. Neither state adds a card above the composer.

A teammate's thread waiting on an ask says who it waits on, using the starter's first name. Its question mark stays in quiet ink. It does not enter Needs you counts, parent counts or attention lines at the list's edge. The person's own asks keep their attention mark.

On phones, New thread places the environment and project selectors side by side above the composer. The project selector opens a searchable vertical sheet with Workspace and the imported projects; model and mode stay inside the composer.

On phones paired with a Mac that lists Orclings, Home floats a bottom bar 62 pt tall: its tabs as glyphs on one Liquid Glass capsule centered on the screen, each tab as wide as fits (52 to 74 pt) with at least 24 pt between them and New, and New at the trailing edge as a round button of the same untinted glass, its plus in ink. New starts what the tab in front makes: a thread on Threads; on Orclings, a native menu that opens from the button with New Orcling and New Thread, where the Mac lets a phone make Orclings, otherwise a thread; on Tasks, the New task screen. New task reads as the task screen does: the title field at the head, then Backlog, a priority menu and a project menu as pills, then the description as Markdown text, with Create task as the ink pill above the keyboard. It saves in a worktree when the Mac's default is one and the project can branch, otherwise in the checkout, and opens the saved task in its place; the draft stays on the phone until then. The chosen tab sits on a quiet ink platter; a touch raises a clear glass lens from it, 30% larger so it rises over the bar's edges, which follows the finger across the tabs with a selection tick at each and chooses the tab it is let go over. The glyph under the lens is drawn above it, grown and in ink. VoiceOver chooses a tab with a double tap, and under Reduce Motion the lens moves without springs. Threads keeps its list under a row of Orcling faces, the first face's body on the wordmark cube's edge, each with an `--attention` dot cut out of the ground at its top right (`--bad` after a failed turn) while its conversation waits on the person or has texts unread; the row has its own switch under This iPhone, on by default, and while it is off the Orclings tab carries a neutral count of the Orclings that need you. Orclings lists each by face, name, newest message and time, newest first, as a messaging app does: the name in ink at 18 pt, the newest message on one faint 16 pt line read as plain text, the time faint at the end of the name's line, an unread dot at the message line's end and a quiet Working tag after the name. Each face moves, built from the flat drawing in layers: it breathes at rest, hops with squash and stretch while its Orcling works, and nudges side to side while it wants the person; its eyes blink and glance on a pace of their own, and glasses follow the glance without blinking. Faces hold still while another tab or a pushed screen covers the list, and under Reduce Motion they are the still drawing. An Orcling's own conversation opens in the chat layout above with the thread's composer, and holding a bubble offers the six tapbacks. Paired with an older Mac, Home keeps the New thread pill and shows no Orclings. Creating and editing Orclings stays on the Mac.

On phones, Home's Threads, Orclings and Tasks lists share one hierarchy, from ink and size alone: titles and names in full ink at 18 pt on one line (a sub thread's at 16 pt), and the line under them faint, so the light theme's close muted and faint inks never carry it; a working thread's title shimmers with a faint band across the ink. The work panel is one opaque plane from the window's top edge to its bottom, its header and page on the same surface. Its tabs are plain labels at natural width that scroll sideways when they no longer fit, and keep their full names; a tab's glyph turns into its close button on hover. Changes is always the first tab, and the plus opens the New tab page. Tool pages start with a 48px bar holding their one ink pill. Web pages keep their own Back/Forward and address pill above an r14 viewport; the agent controls the selected website tab. Files opens on a lazily loaded folder tree with keyboard navigation and remembered expansion; below 580px of file-view width the explorer starts collapsed and opens as an overlay. Explorer dims Git-ignored files and `node_modules` and never lists `.git` or `.DS_Store`. Closing the panel preserves its tabs and running shells. At work-row widths of 788px or less, an open panel overlays the work area.

Tasks, Pull requests and Schedules share one column, 1056 at most (Schedules 860 at its left edge), with no search of their own: the page name, a filter where a well does not cover it, and one lg primary create pill. Rows lead in one 18px slot and end in the person's next move as a key, and only a move the app can make. Group headings count only what waits on the person, in amber. Memory keeps its own search. Settings is a flush plane sheet beside the sidebar, which lists its sections; cards on it are ground cards with hairlines between rows.
