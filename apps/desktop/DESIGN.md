# Design System: OpenOrc desktop

## Overview

First-run setup pairs a column of controls with a flat illustrated collage. Above 900px, the controls align with the window wordmark and the artwork sits toward the right edge, with a flexible gap between them. Navigation and the three-segment progress track stay in a fixed bottom bar. The illustration yields below 720px; setup content scrolls without moving the footer. Its original artwork has a fixed palette. Appearance starts with six palette choices and an expandable catalog; the selected palette stays visible when the catalog closes. An isolated renderer shows the actual app shell and New thread screen at desktop dimensions, then scales the entire view down. Example data and storage stay inside the preview; the selected palette, mode, and saved custom colors update it. Motion is a brief staggered entrance and a token-timed rearrangement between steps, with a settled reduced-motion state. Recovery remains a single-column screen. First-run agent choices form two columns directly on the page, with a logo, stacked name and status, and checkbox; the whole ready row toggles selection. At 400px and narrower, the choices use one column. The default-agent selector follows the choices, so selection precedes the default choice. Setup actions remain visible, while installation metadata stays in Connections. The content supports wheel, trackpad, and keyboard scrolling without a visible scrollbar.

**Creative North Star: "A calm place to think with coding agents"**

OpenOrc is a compact desktop workspace for several projects at once. The sidebar, the conversation, the panel and the page toolbars share one ground. The only lines are the two faint full-height seams beside the conversation and the quieter 1px lines in `--line` that meet them: one under the work column's header and the panel's, one under the sidebar's screens, and one over its footer. Depth is a step in fill, never a line or a card's border. Dark is a black room (#050505): pieces one quiet step up (#101010) with no ring, keys a step above them, soft off-white ink, and menus on a strong hairline and one deep shadow. Light is a warm off-white room (#f5f5f2): pieces and planes in plain white, fills and lines a few percent of the ink over the ground (#ecece9, #e5e5e3), a neutral near-black ink (#171717) whose greys are mixed from it, the ink as the action, and menus on a faint ring and one soft shadow. There is no brand colour; colour is kept for state (diff red and green, needs-you amber, thinking violet). Light is the one accent: the open row, each screen's one ink pill and a ready send button are lit, a faint glow in dark and a soft shadow in light, and a working thread's cube glows. The interface is set at one regular weight, in the system font on a Mac and in Immaculate Gothic on Windows and Linux.

This record describes the built desktop interface. It preserves the workflows in [PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md), including drafts, task authorization, Orcling privacy, and project context. Original artwork and provider marks are documented in [artwork provenance](../../docs/artwork-provenance.md).

**Key Characteristics:**

- One ground everywhere, black or warm off-white; pieces one fill step off it with keys inside them; no rules under headers.
- Hierarchy from title ink, the softer reading ink, muted and faint text, and size, never from weight.
- Round controls: pills for buttons, chips, tracks, and send; 36px rows with r10 corners; planes a soft 14 to 16px.
- Light as the accent: the open row, the one ink pill, a ready send button and a working cube are lit (`--lit`, `--lit-strong`, `--lit-mark`, flat in palettes that do not light them).
- Color only where it means something: diffs, results, attention dots, links, and selection.
- No keyboard shortcut hints and no capability advertising in the UI. The shortcuts themselves still work.

## Colors

The frontmatter records the OpenOrc palette in both modes; component entries show its light values. Components never use these literals. They read semantic roles from `app.css`, so every palette and saved custom color takes the same structure.

### Grounds and fills

`--bg` is the ground, one flat color in every palette, kept opaque because the native window background follows it. `--sidebar` is the sidebar's ground, `--bg` unless a palette tints it. `--well` is a piece on the ground (in light, the palette's plane, opaque in transparency mode; in dark, the ground carved toward black), `--well-ring` its edge (a thin ring and a faint shadow in light, none in dark), and `--key` a control inside a piece (the fill in light). `--surface` is the plane under code, diffs and what floats, and `--raised-shadow` lifts a floating plane (OpenOrc's strong hairline and one soft shadow; an empty shadow elsewhere). `--surface-2` is the fill: hover for bare rows, ghost buttons, and menu items, and the resting fill of chips, tracks, fields, and file heads. `--surface-3` is the strong fill, kept for press, hover on controls that already rest on a fill, switch-off tracks, empty rings, disabled send, and meter tracks. `--line` appears only between rows, at `--hairline` width (half a pixel on high-density screens). Sidebar rows hover on `--navigation-hover`.

**The Contextual Fill Rule.** Selected rows paint `--current-row-fill` and secondary controls paint `--control-fill`. On the tile they are `--navigation-soft` and `--surface`. Every plane (`data-plane`, panel content, menus, dialogs, the composer) restates both as `--surface-2`, and a tile card inside a plane (settings cards, usage accounts, the memory control) restates them back to `--surface`. Mark new planes with `data-plane` instead of growing selector lists.

### Ink and accents

`--ink` is titles and primary text. `--prose-ink` is the agent's reading text, softer than titles in OpenOrc; headings and bold lead-ins stay in `--ink`. `--ink-2` and `--ink-3` are muted, the workhorse. `--ink-4` is faint, for meta, counts, and placeholders; it never sits on `--surface-3`. `--line-number` numbers a diff's lines. Every text role, `--ok` and `--bad` included, holds 4.5:1 on the ground, the sidebar, the plane, the fill and the selected and hovered rows; line numbers hold 3:1 on the ground, the plane and the diff's washes. `--accent` and `--accent-fg` make the one ink pill. Selected and open rows keep `--navigation-ink`, links and inline code use `--content-ink`, so palette accents survive. `--ok` and `--bad` (with soft companions) mark diffs and results, `--attention` colors dots only, and `--warn` is the readable warning text.

Appearance mode and palette selection stay independent. OpenOrc has the values above, Cursor keeps its own grays, and flat light palettes have a hand-tuned ground step so planes still separate. Accent choices and custom colors persist per palette and mode; fill and line roles accept `#rrggbbaa`. Original Kamakura, Cyberpunk, and Eliron themes keep their authored treatment. [Theme references](../../docs/theme-references.md) separate source values from adaptations.

## Typography

The interface starts in the system font on a Mac and in Immaculate Gothic, in its Text 450 face, on Windows and Linux. Settings > Appearance picks it from a list: Immaculate Gothic, Inter, Inter Display, Geist, PP Neue Montreal, or the system font; `fonts.css` maps the choice. Code has its own picker: JetBrains Mono Variable by default, Geist Mono Variable, or the system monospace, applied through the runtime `--font-mono` token to code blocks, editors, diffs, and open terminals.

The interface uses one weight: `--font-weight-medium` and `--font-weight-semibold` are both 400. Bold survives only for author emphasis inside content (replies, task documents, comments). UI text is 14, 15, 16 or 17px; there is no 12px or smaller reading text. The scale, with its tracking, lives in `@theme static`:

- `xs` 14/18 for quiet lines (times, second lines, meta) and code, `sm` 15/18 for section labels, counts and small controls, `base` 16/23 for rows and labels, `md` 17/27 for writing, `header` 17/23 for a column's title in its header, `prose` 17/27 for reading, `lg` 18/25.
- `title-sm` 25/32 for document and page titles, `title` 32/39 for the New thread question and settings page titles, `display` 27/34 for the name heading a profile or a person's page.
- `stat` 21/25 and `stat-sm` 18/23 for numerals, always tabular.

Replies and prompts are 17/27; code is 14. Single-line rows use 18px leading. A rule that sets its own line height reads a `--leading-*` step, named for its Default size, never a px value.

Settings > Appearance also sets the text size. Default is the scale above. Small, in `text-size.css`, is the scale before it grew: each size and leading step two to three pixels down (`xs` 12.5/16, `base` 14/20, `prose` 15/24, `title` 28/34), with spacing and control heights unchanged. Terminals follow it at 12px for Small and 14px for Default. Long prose wraps; code and tables scroll in their own blocks. A table is no piece: it sits on the ground as hairlines under quiet heads, with no fill.

## Layout

The window has one navigation column, a conversation or destination, and an optional work panel. Toolbars are `max(44px, 32px / zoom)` and line up with the traffic lights at x16. The sidebar defaults to 247px, resizes from 224 to 380, and respects saved widths. The panel defaults to 640px within 340 to 900.

The conversation column is at most 800px with 32px reading gutters; the composer uses 24px. Both shrink to 12px at 520px and below and in narrow panes. Each column owns its scrolling. Split conversations keep their own header and panel ownership.

The sidebar is one column, 247px by default (224 to 380), on its `--sidebar` ground; a hairline in `--line` runs the window's full height at its right edge, through its header, and goes while the sidebar floats at 900px and narrower. Hidden, nothing of it stays, and the first header clears the traffic lights. Its 44px header has no line under it and clears the traffic lights where the OS draws them. The sidebar toggle leads the first header beside it, open or hidden. The signed-in space switcher shows OpenOrc in Personal and the organization's mark and name in an organization. Back and Forward sit at the end of the sidebar header when they fit beside the whole name. If they do not fit, they follow the sidebar toggle in the work column's header, as they do while the sidebar is hidden, and return when room allows. One measurement chooses their location, so exactly one pair is shown, dimmed when its history is empty. The name is never cut to make room for the arrows; a name too long for the row alone keeps whole-word clipping and its full-name tooltip. Signed out, the existing plain wordmark and header behavior remain. Under the header New thread leads, a pen in full ink, lit while its screen is open; then the app's screens as named rows, 34px apart, each glyph centred in an 18px lead slot 16px in, on the traffic lights' left edge, and the name at 42: Tasks, Pull requests, Automations and Orclings. There is no Inbox: what waits on the person shows where it lives. A count sits at a row's end only where something waits: on Tasks, tasks whose run asks for an approval, tasks proposed and tasks in review, each once; on Orclings, Orcling chats with a question or a failure. Every count in the sidebar is amber, centred in a 16px slot on the line the row marks share, and counts nothing else; there are no grey totals. A thread waiting on an approval or a question carries its mark on its own row and counts on its project's label and on the folded Workspaces head, however old its work, since a thread that waits never goes to Resolved by age. Orclings is a toggle that swaps the threads for the Orclings list and back; while it does, its row carries New group chat and New Orcling at its end, and the list, with no head of its own, starts 18px under the screens. Memory is reached from the right panel and the palette. While Settings is open, its head takes New thread's place: a chevron in the third ink beside the name Settings in full ink, one button back to the screen Settings was opened from, centred between two 1px lines in `--line` that run the sidebar's width, one under the header and one over the sections. The upper one lies on the header's last pixel, so it and the line under the work column's header read as one line across the window. Its sections take the screens' and threads' place and scroll under the head. The footer's gear becomes a house while Settings is open, and leaves it the same way. The 44px footer, with no line over it, holds the update while one waits and Settings at the row's end. Its start is the person's: a 36px row, signed in with a 26px face and two lines, the name at 14/16 and the plan at 12/14, which says the hours alone where "Pro · 41 h left" would not fit; signed out, Sign in, drawn as a screen row (a person glyph 16px in, the word in the third ink), which opens OpenOrc's sign-in page in the browser. While the page waits it reads "Signing in…" and its glyph turns, a cross under the pointer that calls the sign-in off; a sign-in that failed says why on a second line in the bad ink, cut to the width with the whole of it on hover. A build with no account server shows nothing there, and Settings has no Account section.

Scoped views keep project groups, counts, search and task lists within the selected space. An organization contains the local folders paired with its usable cloud projects and its readable cloud threads; Personal keeps unmatched folders and personal cloud work. A cloud project without a local folder uses the cloud mark and offers OpenOrc Cloud only. The organization filter adds Mine and Everyone's above the existing thread filters, without a heading. ## Elevation & Depth

Depth is the fill step, lifted by `--raised-shadow` where the step alone is too faint. Persistent surfaces have no borders. Planes are one step from the ground, fills one step from theirs.

**The Floating Fill Rule.** Menus, popovers, tooltips, toasts, and dialogs have no border and wear `--raised-shadow`. They paint `--float-fill`: the plane when the layer lands on the tile or on a piece set into it (the composer, a card, a Settings tile), with a hairline ring in `--line-strong` over a piece; the tile when it lands on a plane, such as a sheet, a dialog, the panel or another menu (`use-anchor-ground` sets `data-ground` on the portal). Dialogs also dim their backdrop. A layer that lies across both, such as a composer picker over the conversation or an update toast over the transcript, has no fill step away from both, so a hairline ring in `--line-strong` draws its edge.

A select's list is the one exception. macOS draws it natively; Windows and Linux draw it in the page (`styles/controls.css`), dressed as that native menu so a select reads the same on every OS: `--surface` at 85% behind a blur (solid under Reduce Transparency), a hairline edge, `--shadow-overlay`, and the accent on one row, the focused one, which the pointer and the arrow keys both move.

**The Opaque Work Plane Rule.** Keep reading, writing, and tool content opaque when Transparent surroundings is on. The setting paints the shell tint once through `--shell-opacity`, from 0% (solid tint) to 100% (native blur), defaulting to 35%. System Reduce Transparency wins.

## Shapes

Corner radius is set per component:

- 6: labels and diff runs.
- 10: rows and menu items.
- 12: tall rows, sheet fields, and diff file heads.
- 14: the browser viewport.
- 14: drop menus, command, schedule, and toast cards, code blocks.
- 16: popovers, prompt bubbles, permission, task, and MCP cards, settings cards, the palette, sheets, dialogs, grown menus (model, branch, context, slash), steps and question cards, the conversation composer, the commit sheet and usage account cards.
- 18: the New thread composer.
- Full: buttons, chips, tracks, and send.

Control sizes: Button sm 28, md 30, lg 32, xl 36, hero 40. IconButton 24, 28, 30, or 32, always round. Chips and badges 22, rows 36, send 32 (34 on New thread).

Icons are [Precision Outline](../../docs/precision-outline-icons.md): a 24-unit grid, 1.75 strokes, rounded joins and caps, `currentColor`, from `components/icons.tsx`. Filled collaboration marks, the Hollow conversation indicator, and upstream framework logos remain deliberate exceptions. Orclings keep their own shapes and colors.

## Motion

Motion uses tokens only: leaving 160ms; arriving 220ms with opacity, a 4px blur, and a 4px rise; a 40ms stagger capped near eight rows; confirmations with one overshoot over 260 to 360ms; shape resizes over 420ms. Menus open out of the control that was touched, and states swap in place. History loads and remounts never animate. Under reduced motion, every duration and delay is zero and every keyframe ends visible.

## Components

### Controls

Use the shared primitives in `components/ui.tsx`, `Segmented.tsx`, and `menu.ts`. Primary is the ink pill, secondary and danger rest on `--control-fill` (danger with a `--bad` label), ghost is bare with a fill hover. `hover-step` layers the fill step over a control's own fill so hover reads on tile and plane. Segmented controls are a fill track at natural width with a surface knob that slides over 320ms. Switches are an ink track when on and `--surface-3` when off. ## Do's and Don'ts

- Do read semantic roles and the contextual fills; never paste OpenOrc literals into components.
- Do keep one ink pill per screen and let everything else rest on fills.
- Do keep every existing control and saved state; restyle, don't remove.
- Do preserve saved widths, palettes, custom colors, keyboard access, native window clearance, drafts, and Orcling privacy.
- Don't add borders to persistent surfaces, or any shadow but `--raised-shadow` on what floats, a piece's `--well-ring`, and the floating sidebar's overlay.
- Don't use bold, 11px text, or faint text on the strong fill.
- Don't show shortcut hints or advertise what a control can do; reasons belong on disabled controls, on demand.
- Don't ship a control that does nothing.

Source authority is `src/renderer/src/app.css`, `workspace-design.css`, `settings-design.css`, `styles/*.css`, the theme modules, and the production components. The direction contract is the first HTML comment in `src/renderer/index.html`.

For a safe visual preview, run `pnpm preview:design` from the repository root and open `http://127.0.0.1:5177/?view=workspace&theme=light`. `OPENORC_PREVIEW_PORT` changes the port. The preview renders production components with synthetic providers and session-only settings; it does not verify live provider actions or real Orcling services.

Account shows only the signed-in person's selected paid allowance and cloud hours from the server. Its source reads Paid by you or Paid by [organization]. During grace, the exact private reason and server deadline appear beside the plan, and the footer replaces hours with Pro ends today/tomorrow at [time], using the short local date when narrow. At a local deadline without a fresh billing read, Cloud hours says Not available and offers no hours purchase; a confirmed total after losing Pro remains authoritative, including zero. The explicit no_pro reason keeps a never-paid Free account without bought hours on its existing rows, with no hours row. Its existing Plan hint stays unless the person belongs to an organization, when the exact private reason explains the blocked access. A seat-covered person may expand Pay for Pro yourself into the existing personal checkout controls; payment waiting, reopening and stopping keep their existing meaning.

Cloud Changes uses Branch for the saved comparison and keeps Commit and an existing pull request link in their local positions. Push and pull request creation stay in Commits. Only server uncommitted work enables Commit; without a server dirty stat, its summary row says Uncommitted changes and never substitutes cumulative totals. Disabled cloud controls expose their specific reason on hover and keyboard focus.

Cloud helper agents reuse the existing subthread tree and read-only conversation. Each child shows its own persisted history and status. Selected Stop requires the current server capability and host handle; unsupported Stop explains itself only on hover or focus. A pending or failed history read never appears as empty history. Unknown composer context shows nothing, and an uncounted Commit chip says only Commit.

Human conversations use their own organization-scoped tabs, preserve the work pane and editor state, and never enter AgentEvent or Orcling run streams. A chat replaces only a pristine New thread placeholder. Words, an attachment or a chosen project keep that tab open beside the chat. Ordinary work-thread navigation keeps its existing behavior.

Channel previews keep earlier history readable and replace the composer with Join channel. Private channels appear only to their members; an admin has no separate entry. Details use the server's current add and remove permissions. Public removal restores preview and preserves the draft; private removal clears content and keeps the tab's place with Close chat. Channel dialogs fit a short window without hiding their primary action.

Organization content uses one shared access gate and frozen screen. The server's grace deadline closes protected content even while offline; cached bodies and rosters clear before the screen changes, while private drafts survive a freeze. The frozen work area keeps the organization's identity, its 40px mark, a 28/34 title, the exact private access sentence and Upgrade to Pro leading to Account. It exposes no project list, transcript, composer or agent header. Personal work stays available; membership departure is distinct from a temporary loss of Pro.

Unsupported cloud controls keep their existing position and explain their specific reason only on hover or keyboard focus. An already open unavailable panel contains the disabled control, without a false empty state or a permanent limitations paragraph. Missing routes on a readable older server use the same treatment; failed reads remain failures with Retry. Context values come from reported events, and unknown context has no percentage. The sidebar usage control never uses a Mac account allowance for a cloud thread; unavailable cloud usage keeps its glyph disabled with the reason on demand. Cloud history search cannot display cached Mac matches while its execution place is loading. Local controls and layout stay unchanged.

Organization admins have Seats beside People, with one shared identity and a saved section per space. A submitted dialog says Close; an uncertain response keeps its choices for an explicit retry. A completed purchase removes that action even when the following read fails. Storage appears as one quiet used/allowance line beneath the Seats summary, using the server's totals. Its independent read never blocks the inventory; unread totals are omitted, and uploads or seat changes trigger a fresh read.
